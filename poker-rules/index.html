No-Limit Texas Hold'em

Home Cash Game Rules

These rules are intended to keep the game friendly, fair, clear, and easy to run.

1. General Conduct

Repeated failure to follow the rules may result in a player being asked to cash out.

Players are expected to treat each other respectfully.

The host will handle all shuffling, dealing, pot management, and rulings.

2. Seating

When all players have arrived, seating order and the first dealer button will be randomized.

Seats remain fixed throughout the event.

3. Buy-In, Rebuy, and Cash-Out

When entering the game, a player may buy in for up to $40.

After a hand, if a player has less than $20 in chips, they may rebuy an additional $20 in chips, or if they have no chips left, they may rebuy $40 in chips.

Across the initial buy-in and all rebuys combined, a player may not buy more than $80 in chips during the event.

Only chips on the table at the start of a deal are in play for that hand. Rebuys may only be made between hands.

A player may cash out after any hand. Once a player cashes out, they may not rejoin for the rest of the event.

4. Game Structure

The game is no-limit Texas hold'em. Each player makes the best possible five-card poker hand using any combination of their two hole cards and the five community cards. Hands are ranked from strongest to weakest as follows:

  1. Royal flush
  2. Straight flush
  3. Four of a kind
  4. Full house
  5. Flush
  6. Straight
  7. Three of a kind
  8. Two pair
  9. One pair
  10. High card

Only the best five-card hand matters; extra cards outside a player's best five-card hand do not count.

If two or more players have the same hand category, the winner is determined by the highest relevant cards within the best five-card hand. For example, a higher pair beats a lower pair, and if both players have the same pair, the highest kicker may decide the winner. If two players have a full house, the higher 3 of a kind determines the winner first, then the higher pair.

Suits do not affect hand strength except when they are used to make a flush, straight flush, or royal flush. For example, if two players each have a pair of aces, one player does not win simply because they have the ace of spades.

If two or more players have exactly equal best five-card hands, the pot is split between those players.

5. Blinds

The blinds are $0.25/$0.25.

The small blind and big blind both have the option to raise if no one else has raised before action reaches them.

No straddles are allowed.

6. Action

Players must act in turn.

A player who acts out of turn will be warned by the host, but if their action is still legal when it is their turn to act, it will be binding.

Any player may request that the active player be put on a 60-second clock. If the active player does not act before the clock expires, their hand is dead.

7. Betting

All checks, bets, calls, raises, folds, and all-ins must be verbal. Pushing chips forward or tapping the table without a verbal declaration does not count as an action.

Verbal declarations are binding once made, as long as the declared action is legal.

If a player declares an illegal bet or action, the host will inform them and give them an opportunity to make a legal action.

No string betting is allowed. A bet or raise amount must be declared clearly in one continuous statement. Examples of string betting include: “I call your $1 and raise $3,” or “I'll bet one dollar… and seventy-five cents,” with a noticeable pause.

A player should place their bet in front of them in the betting area after verbally declaring it. Do not splash chips directly into the pot. The host will pull bets into the pot after the betting round is complete.

Allow the host to make change from the pot when needed. Do not ask other players to make change during a hand unless the host directs it.

There is no one-chip rule. A single chip placed forward does not automatically mean a call, bet, or raise. The verbal declaration controls the action.

There is no 50% rule. An incomplete raise is not automatically rounded up or treated as a full raise unless the player clearly declares a valid raise amount.

8. Raises, All-Ins, and Side Pots

The minimum raise is the size of the previous bet or raise. For example, if one player bets $2, the minimum raise is to $4. If another player then raises from $2 to $5, that is a $3 raise, so the next minimum raise is to $8.

A player may go all-in at any time, even if it is less than the minimum raise.

An all-in bet that is less than a full legal raise does not reopen betting to players who have already acted.

When a player goes all-in, other players bets may form side pots. A player can only win any pots they contributed equally to.

9. Showdown

A player must show their cards to be eligible to win any portion of any pot. A player who folds or mucks their cards without showing them cannot win the pot, even if they may have had the best hand.

A player is allowed to fold or muck their cards during the showdown, forfeiting their claim to any portion of the pot, for example after they have seen the strength of another player's hand.

If multiple players want to see other players' cards before showing their own, the host may instruct players to show their cards starting with the most recent aggressor, or in table order if there was no action.

In a split pot, odd large chips will be broken down into smaller denominations. If there are still odd chips of the smallest denomination after the pot is split as evenly as possible, one odd chip goes to the each winning hand closest to the button, moving clockwise.

No rabbit hunting: if a hand ends before all board cards are dealt, the undealt board cards will not be revealed.

Hands will only be run once.

10. Cards and Misdeals

Players are responsible for protecting their own cards at all times.

Cards must remain on the table at all times.

If a player's cards are abandoned, touch the muck, or released face-down toward the muck, the hand will be treated as folded.

If a single hole card is accidentally exposed during the initial deal, after the other cards are dealt, that player will be dealt a replacement card and the exposed card will become the first burn card. If two hole cards are accidentally exposed, the hand will be treated as a misdeal and will be redealt.

If a community card is accidentally exposed, either at the wrong time or by dealing too many cards, the exposed cards will be shuffled back into the remaining deck stub.

If a burn card is accidentally exposed at the correct time, that card will remain the burn card.

11. Away Players, Missed Blinds, and Button Movement

At least four players must be present and eligible to receive cards for a hand to be dealt. If fewer than four players are present, the game will be paused.

After a hand, this procedure will be followed:

  1. Remove the players who are leaving and insert the players who are joining. The host may enforce that the players decide to join going clockwise from the big blind.
  2. Move the big blind button clockwise to the next present player.
  3. Move the small blind button clockwise to the player closest to the new big blind, or to the empty seat closest to the new big blind if there are no present players between the old small blind and the new big blind.
  4. Move the dealer button clockwise to the player closest to the new small blind, or to the empty seat closest to the new small blind if there are no present players between the old dealer and the new small blind.
  5. Mark any players or seats which missed a blind button.
  6. The player with the big blind button posts only a live big blind.
  7. The player with the small blind button posts a live small blind, or up to one missed big blind live and one small blind dead.
  8. Any other joining player posts up to one missed big blind live and one missed small blind dead.

12. Collusion

No collusion, chip dumping, soft play by agreement, or private deals between players are allowed. This includes intentionally losing hands, intentionally transferring chips, or playing differently to help another player. A player may, however, buy chips for another player.

During a hand, players may not disclose, imply, hint at, or discuss the contents of any live or folded hand.

During a hand, players may not seek or offer advice to another player or outside source.
