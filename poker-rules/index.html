No-Limit Texas Hold'em

Home Cash Game Rules

These rules are intended to keep the game friendly, fair, clear, and easy to run.

1. General Conduct

Repeated failure to follow the rules may result in a player being asked to cash out.

Players are expected to treat each other respectfully.

No collusion, chip dumping, soft play by agreement, or private deals between players are allowed. This includes intentionally losing hands, intentionally transferring chips, or playing differently to help another player. A player may, however, buy chips for another player.

During a hand, players may not disclose, imply, hint at, or discuss the contents of any live or folded hand.

During a hand, players may not seek advice from another player or outside source.

During a hand, players may not advise, criticize, coach, or discuss another player's decision or strategy.

No rabbit hunting. If a hand ends before all board cards are dealt, the undealt board cards will not be revealed.

Hands will only be run once.

2. Host, Seating, Dealer, and Starting the Game

The host will handle all shuffling, dealing, pot management, and rulings.

When all players have arrived, seating order and the first dealer button will be randomized.

Seats remain fixed throughout the event.

At least four players must be present and eligible to receive cards for a hand to be dealt.

3. Blinds and Game Structure

The game is no-limit Texas hold'em.

The blinds are $0.25/$0.25.

The small blind and big blind both have the option to raise if no one else has raised before action reaches them.

No straddles are allowed.

4. Buy-In, Rebuy, and Cash-Out Rules

When entering the game, a player may buy in for up to $40.

After a hand, if a player has less than $20 in chips, they may buy an additional $20 in chips.

If that player later has less than $20 again, they may buy an additional $20 one final time. Each player may make a maximum of two $20 rebuys.

Only chips on the table at the start of a deal are in play for that hand. If rebuys or add-ons are allowed, they may only be made between hands.

A player may cash out after any hand. Once a player cashes out, they may not rejoin for the rest of the event.

5. Cards, Misdeals, and Hand Protection

Players are responsible for protecting their own cards at all times.

Cards must remain on the table at all times.

If a player's cards are abandoned or clearly released face-down toward the muck, the hand will be treated as folded.

If any card from a player's hand touches any part of the muck, that hand is dead.

If any hole card is accidentally exposed during the deal, the hand is a misdeal and will be redealt.

If a community card or burn card is accidentally exposed at the wrong time, that card will be shuffled back into the deck and redealt.

6. Acting in Turn and the Clock

Players must act in turn.

Any player may request that the active player be put on a 60-second clock. If the active player does not act before the clock expires, their hand is dead.

7. Betting Procedure

All bets, calls, raises, folds, and all-ins must be verbal. Pushing chips forward without a verbal declaration does not count as an action.

Verbal declarations are binding once made, as long as the declared action is legal.

If a player declares an illegal bet or action, the host will inform them and give them an opportunity to make a legal action.

No string betting is allowed. A bet or raise amount must be declared clearly in one continuous statement. Examples of string betting include: “I call your $1 and raise $3,” or “I'll bet one dollar… and seventy-five cents,” with a noticeable pause.

A player should place their bet in front of them in the betting area after verbally declaring it. Do not splash chips directly into the pot. The host will pull bets into the pot after the betting round is complete.

Allow the host to make change from the pot when needed. Do not ask other players to make change during a hand unless the host directs it.

There is no one-chip rule. A single chip placed forward does not automatically mean a call, bet, or raise. The verbal declaration controls the action.

There is no 50% rule. An incomplete raise is not automatically rounded up or treated as a full raise unless the player clearly declares a valid raise amount.

8. Raises, All-Ins, and Side Pots

The minimum raise is the size of the previous bet or raise.

For example, if one player bets $2, the minimum raise is to $4. If another player then raises from $2 to $5, that is a $3 raise, so the next minimum raise is to $8.

An all-in bet that is less than a full legal raise does not reopen betting to players who have already acted.

All-in bets may create side pots. A player can only win the main pot and any side pot they are eligible for based on the amount they put in.

9. Away Players, Missed Blinds, and Button Movement

A player who is away from the table is not dealt cards.

If a player is away and misses the small blind, big blind, or both, the host will mark which blind or blinds they missed.

When any players are away, the next big blind moves clockwise to the next present player. The small blind and dealer button are the two seats immediately to the right of the big blind, even if one or both of those seats belongs to an away player.

A returning player who missed blinds may rejoin in one of two ways:

  1. They may wait until they are next assigned the big blind, post the normal live big blind, and play the hand normally.
  2. They may rejoin when they are not in the small blind by posting missed blinds. They owe at most one missed big blind live and one missed small blind dead, no matter how many orbits they missed.

The live big blind counts as that player's preflop bet.

The dead small blind goes directly into the pot and does not count as that player's preflop bet.

A returning player may not rejoin as the small blind.

10. Determining the Winner

In Texas hold'em, each player makes the best possible five-card poker hand using any combination of their two hole cards and the five community cards.

Hands are ranked from strongest to weakest as follows:

  1. Royal flush
  2. Straight flush
  3. Four of a kind
  4. Full house
  5. Flush
  6. Straight
  7. Three of a kind
  8. Two pair
  9. One pair
  10. High card

Only the best five-card hand matters. Extra cards outside a player's best five-card hand do not count.

If two or more players have the same hand category, the winner is determined by the highest relevant cards within the best five-card hand. For example, a higher pair beats a lower pair, and if both players have the same pair, the highest kicker may decide the winner. If two players have a full house, the higher 3 of a kind determines the winner first, then the higher pair.

Suits do not affect hand strength except when they are used to make a flush, straight flush, or royal flush. For example, if two players each have a pair of aces, one player does not win simply because they have the ace of spades. The winner is determined by the best five-card hand, including any relevant kickers.

If two or more players have exactly equal best five-card hands, the pot is split between those players.

11. Showdown, Split Pots, and Odd Chips

A player must show their cards to be eligible to win any portion of any pot. A player who folds or mucks their cards without showing them cannot win the pot, even if they may have had the best hand.

At showdown, the host may instruct players to show their cards starting with the most recent aggressor, or in table order if there was no action.

In a split pot, large chips will be broken down to the smallest denomination used in the game. If there is still an odd chip after the pot is split as evenly as possible, the odd chip goes to the winning hand closest to the button, moving clockwise.
